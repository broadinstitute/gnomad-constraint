Summary

  • The port is exact, and rr is applied after calibration. Given the notebook's training data, the pipeline reproduces syn_adj_r to within 5×10⁻¹³. Expected counts are multiplied by rr only when per-variant expected counts are aggregated; the plateau and coverage models are read unchanged.
  • Retrained on the combined synonymous DNM set, the new rr is close to legacy syn_adj_r but slightly weaker. With the overall level removed, dispersion is 4.45 for the new rr and 4.37 for legacy, against 5.08 with no correction. Outliers are about the same (214–219 for the new rr vs 213–219 for legacy, from 254–270 with no correction). The gap comes from the training set, not the code.
  • Normalization only changes the overall level, not the noise. Raw rr averages about 1.014 over exome synonymous sites, so it raises every gene's expected by about 1.4%. That makes raw dispersion look 0.12 lower, but with the level removed raw and normalized rr are the same (legacy 4.378 vs 4.373; new 4.455 vs 4.447). Without normalization, synonymous obs/exp in constrained genes (LOEUF deciles 0–5) drops to 0.986–0.998. Normalization should stay on.
  • Clipping to [0.2, 5] has no effect: almost no window falls outside that range.
  • chrX/Y: rr helps slightly unmasked, and masking removes that. Over 769 chrX/Y transcripts, level-free dispersion is 4.36 with no correction, 4.33 with unmasked rr, and 4.43 with masking. Legacy has no chrX/Y rr.
  • At full strength, the Bonferroni cutoff (the legacy rule) is the best feature-selection cutoff; looser cutoffs model more contexts but overfit. With a fitted strength, looser cutoffs give the lowest dispersion (section 11).
  • Both rr versions are about 25–30% too strong in coding sequence. Their calibration slopes are 0.68 (new) and 0.75 (legacy), and dispersion is lowest at about 0.75 strength. The non-coding adj_r used in PR #55 is about 3–4× too strong (slope 0.28) and inflates expected counts by about 9%.
  • Retraining on the Satterstrom 2026 DNMs (23,336 DNMs, +37%) makes calibration worse at the Bonferroni cutoff (section 8). The new files extend the old DDD+ASD set, and the model predicts held-out DNMs better (gain 86 → 132), but core-set dispersion rises by 0.14 ± 0.01. The new rr achieves 53% of legacy's gain on the core set instead of 88%, and 69% instead of 90% over all transcripts. Only about 20% of the rr change carries over to gnomAD. At the 1e-4 cutoff calibration is nearly unchanged (+0.013 ± 0.009 out-of-fold), making it the best cutoff with the new DNMs at full strength; with a fitted strength, looser cutoffs do better (section 11).
  • Fitting the rr strength recovers most of that loss (section 9). Every rr is too strong at full strength; chosen on held-out chromosomes, α is 0.625 for the new Bonferroni rr and 0.75 for the old. At fitted strength the new set reaches 99% of legacy's full-strength gain, and is behind the old set by 0.035 ± 0.008. The loss is in non-CpG contexts. The odds-ratio rr scale and separate CpG/non-CpG strengths do not help with either training set.
  • Outliers barely separate the good options; dispersion does (sections 10–11). rr removes about 50 synonymous outliers (270 → 217–222); the best options are within bootstrap noise of each other, and the ~130 deficit outliers are the same under every option.
  • Recommended: the new Satterstrom 2026 DNMs with the 1e-2 cutoff at strength α ≈ 0.625 (section 11, Recommendation). Out-of-fold it has the same synonymous outliers as the old Bonferroni rr at full strength (222 vs 222) with 0.10 lower dispersion, and with all-chromosome models it is slightly less noisy than legacy syn_adj_r. It needs a strength setting in --compute-rr; without one, the old DDD+ASD set with Bonferroni at full strength gives the same outliers.
  • The earlier adj_r report agrees on every finding measured in both (normalization, the strength of adj_r and syn_adj_r, outlier reduction). Its suggestion to loosen the feature-selection cutoff works only together with its other suggestion, fitting the strength (section 11).
  • These results settle the synonymous comparison. The constraint pipeline still has to be run for LoF and missense metrics (LOEUF, pLI, flags); see the last section.

Background

  • rr is Gnocchi's regional mutation-rate ratio (Chen et al. 2023, atgu/gnomad_nc_constraint). For each of the 32 strand-collapsed trinucleotide contexts, a logistic model separates de novo mutation (DNM) sites from non-mutated sites using 13 regional genomic features, each at 4 flanking scales (1 kb–1 Mb). For a 1 kb window, rr = σ(β₀ + β·z(x)) / σ(β₀): the model's DNM probability for the window relative to the average window. Expected counts are multiplied by rr.
  • Legacy tables (gs://gnomad/v4.1/constraint/resources/annotations/ht/):
    • adj_r: Gnocchi's released models, trained on genome-wide (mostly non-coding) DNMs.
    • syn_adj_r: the same machinery refit on synonymous DNMs in syn_dnm_adj_r_methods.ipynb. It has models for 12 of 32 contexts and covers autosomes only.
  • What the previous report found: adj_r is scaled to the genome while our expected counts are calibrated on the exome, so it over-scales coding sites by about 11%, and it is also about 4× too strong there. syn_adj_r was well calibrated but covered only 12 contexts and was built by hand.
  • This work builds syn_adj_r into the pipeline, applies it per variant after model calibration, extends it to chrX/Y, and makes the feature-selection cutoff tunable. This report compares each change against legacy syn_adj_r on the same exome synonymous data.

Data and metrics

  • DNMs: 17,735 synonymous DNMs across cohorts (DDD+ASD 14,283, deCODE+PsychENCODE 1,876, CHD 642, gnomAD v4.1 430, CDH 228, OFC 193, EE 83). Deduplicating removes 676 sites reported by two or more cohorts (652 are shared by DDD+ASD and deCODE+PsychENCODE, which share samples), leaving 17,059 unique DNMs. That is the same variant set as the notebook's input (0 differences). 202 are on chrX and none on chrY. The notebook dropped chrX and kept the duplicates.
  • Training set: 17,059 DNMs and 479,021 background sites, versus 17,533 DNM rows (with duplicates) and 459,612 background sites for legacy.
  • Features: the published 13-feature 1 kb matrix (chr1–22) plus the new chrX/Y matrix (213,267 windows). On chrX/Y, 7 of the 13 features have source data; the other 6 are missing.
  • Evaluation set: synonymous sites of 18,406 Ensembl MANE Select transcripts in the v4.1.1 per-variant expected table. For each transcript, adj observed is compared with unadjusted expected × rr, with rr = 1 where a site has no window or the context has no model. This is the same calculation the pipeline's aggregation step performs.
  • Metrics:
    • Raw dispersion: mean z² over all transcripts, where z = (obs − exp)/√exp.
    • Outliers: |z| > 8.
    • Level-free dispersion: each variant's expected counts are first rescaled so overall obs/exp = 1, so this measures noise only. The core set is the 17,032 unflagged transcripts with expected ≥ 20, the previous report's Test 9 definition.
    • Calibration slope: the previous report's Test 8, on 16,458 unflagged transcripts with expected ≥ 50. It is the log-log slope of uncorrected obs/exp against the gene's mean rr; 1 means rr matches the observed variation exactly.
    • o/e by LOEUF decile: fixed v4.1.2 LOEUF deciles.
    • Absolute values differ from the previous report's (for example its no-correction dispersion of 2.58), which used v4.1.2 expected counts on autosomal transcripts only, so compare variants within this report.
  • How the variants are built: regional_rate_pipeline.py --compare-rr-variants builds each variant from the trained models with the same code as --compute-rr, so each change can be switched on separately.

2. Normalization

Expected-weighted mean rr over exome synonymous sites, by context. These are the factors that normalization divides by. Only contexts with a model in either version are shown; the rest are exactly 1.

  • Both versions average above 1 in coding sequence (legacy 1.011, new 1.014 per gene), most for ACC, GCT, and TCT (up to 1.09). The non-coding adj_r averages 1.094 (up to 1.32 for CCC). Our expected counts are already calibrated on exome synonymous sites, so this excess counts the exome's above-average rate twice.
  • Raw rr looks better only because of the overall level. Uncorrected obs/exp is above 1 overall (1.015; 1.011 among unflagged genes with expected ≥ 20), mostly because of the least-constrained genes. Raw rr's roughly 1.4% increase brings the average to about 1 (1.001 and 0.998). But it is nearly the same for every gene, so it does this by pushing constrained genes below 1 (section 3), and the level-free numbers show it removes no extra noise. An overall offset like this belongs in the model calibration, not in rr.
  • Normalization keeps constrained genes at 1 (next section), and it keeps LoF and missense expected counts at the level the models were calibrated to. Without it, LoF expected would also rise by about 1.4% and shift LOEUF for every gene.

  • The new DNMs mainly change which features are selected. With 37% more DNMs, weak associations cross the Bonferroni threshold: 26 of the old 34 features are kept, 8 are dropped, and 16 are added, mostly GC- and methylation-related features in non-CpG contexts (met_sperm in ACA and ACT; CpG_island in CCA, GCC, TCA, TCC; GC_content in CCA and CCT).   • The new DNMs are not obviously different. Against background sites of the same context, the added DNMs differ from the retained ones by at most |t| ≈ 3 across about 50 feature-scale tests. Larger differences are between older cohorts: SSC and CHD 2017 DNMs are depleted in GC-rich and CpG-island windows (about −0.11 to −0.19 SD), consistent with older exome captures detecting fewer DNMs there.
  • Why DNM-predictive features might not carry over (not tested): gnomAD expected counts already model CpG methylation and coverage, which correlate with these features; gnomAD counts reflect polymorphism, which GC-biased gene conversion and weak selection also shape; and exome DNM detection varies regionally in a different way from gnomAD calls.

9. Why the new DNMs do worse: rr scale, CpG vs non-CpG, and strength

A temporary script (not part of the pipeline) rebuilt rr from the trained models of both training sets for many variants at once and scored them on the same 18,406 transcripts. It reproduces the pipeline exactly (per-transcript expected identical on the test genes; mean z² 4.7297 vs 4.7298 and 4.5770 vs 4.5792 on the full set, the small differences being clipping). All variants here are normalized per context, use models trained on all chromosomes, and do not mask chrX/Y. Clipping has since been removed from the pipeline (it changed dispersion by less than 0.003). Values are core-set level-free dispersion (no correction 2.908; lower is better).

Odds-ratio scale

rr = σ(β₀ + βz) / σ(β₀) is compressed when DNMs are a large fraction of a context's training rows (up to 45% for ACG), which applies to the CpG contexts, and the compression depends on the DNM-to-background ratio. The odds-ratio scale, rr = exp(βz), removes that dependence.

Where the two reports differ:

  • Loosening the feature-selection cutoff (supported only with a fitted strength). The earlier report suggested refitting syn_adj_r with a less strict cutoff so that more than 12 contexts get a correction. At full strength, looser cutoffs overfit (worse dispersion, slopes about 0.4; sections 6 and 8). Combined with the earlier report's other suggestion, fitting the strength, they give the lowest dispersion of any option (section 11). The two suggestions work together but not separately.
  • "Well calibrated" vs "25–30% too strong" (same data). The earlier report's slope of 0.70 and best α of 0.75 are what this report describes as too strong. Either way the gain from fitting the strength is small (about 2% of dispersion).
  • Fitting the strength (new caveat). The earlier report proposed fitting α and measured only dispersion. For the Bonferroni rr, a lower α improves dispersion but adds about 8 synonymous outliers (section 10); for looser cutoffs, a lower α improves both (section 11).
  • Untested explanations, now tested. "A ratio of probabilities from a case/control training set is not a rate ratio": true for CpG contexts, but the odds-ratio scale gives the same result once the strength is fitted and is worse at full strength (section 9). "Features learned from mostly non-coding DNMs may not carry over to exons": even features learned from synonymous DNMs carry over only partly, and weak ones about 20% (sections 8–9).

Do the downstream constraint steps need to be run?

  • Not for the synonymous comparison. These metrics use the same per-variant expected counts and the same per-variant × rr multiplication that --aggregate-per-variant-expected uses (check 5 confirms the pipeline gives the same numbers). Running the constraint pipeline wouldn't change any synonymous conclusion here.
  • Yes, for everything else. rr also changes LoF and missense expected counts, so LOEUF and its ranks and deciles, pLI, missense and LoF z-scores, the outlier flags, and the sd_raw_z scaling all need the full run (--aggregate-per-variant-expected through --prepare-release). LOEUF-based checks, such as how well LOEUF still separates haploinsufficient genes, need those outputs.
  • A separate no-rr run isn't needed: the release keeps the unadjusted results in unadj, which equal a no-rr run exactly (check 5).
  • Settle the open choices first (training set and cutoff, chrX/Y masking, and possibly rr strength), so the full run is done once with the final rr table.

Conclusions

  1. The code is correct. It reproduces legacy syn_adj_r exactly on legacy inputs, and rr is applied after calibration without touching the models.
  2. The new rr matches legacy closely and adds chrX/Y and a tunable cutoff. It is slightly less effective (about 90% of legacy's noise reduction) because of the new training set.
  3. Normalization is right and costs nothing in noise. Its apparent cost in raw dispersion is a 1.4% overall offset that would otherwise also shift LoF and missense expected counts.
  4. chrX/Y masking hurts. Unmasked rr is slightly better than none on chrX/Y; masked rr is worse.
  5. Both synonymous rr versions are about 25–30% too strong in coding sequence; the non-coding adj_r is about 3–4× too strong.
  6. More DNMs help only if the strength is fitted. With the Satterstrom 2026 DNMs, the Bonferroni rr at full strength predicts DNMs better but gnomAD synonymous counts worse, because the newly selected weak features are overstated. With a looser cutoff and a fitted strength the new DNMs are as good as the old (section 11).
  7. rr strength is the setting that matters. All synonymous rr versions, including legacy, are best at α ≈ 0.625–0.875, and a fitted strength makes the two training sets nearly equivalent. The rr scale and per-group strengths are not worth adding.
  8. Cutoff and strength have to be chosen together. For the Bonferroni rr, a lower strength fits the bulk of genes better but leaves a few more outliers; for looser cutoffs, a fitted strength improves both. The best balance is a looser cutoff at α ≈ 0.625.

Recommendation

With the lowest synonymous outlier rate as the priority (revised on 2026-10-21 after section 11; the earlier version recommended the old DDD+ASD set with Bonferroni at full strength):

  1. Outliers do not separate the good options, so choose among them on dispersion. rr removes about 50 synonymous outliers. Out-of-fold, the best options have 219–226 outliers, and their differences from the old Bonferroni rr at full strength (222) are within the bootstrap intervals (about ±8).
  2. Use the new Satterstrom 2026 DNMs with the 1e-2 cutoff at strength α ≈ 0.625, normalized, with chrX/Y unmasked. Out-of-fold it has the same outliers as the old Bonferroni rr at full strength (222 vs 222, +0 [−8, +8]) with 0.104 ± 0.012 lower dispersion, as good as the old Bonferroni rr at its own best strength. With all-chromosome models it is slightly less noisy than legacy syn_adj_r (−0.013 ± 0.008) with +3 [−3, +9] outliers, uses the newer cohorts, and covers chrX/Y.
  3. Code needed: a strength setting in --compute-rr (rrα before normalization, recorded in the rr Table globals), and the 1e-2 cutoff passed to --compute-rr and the constraint pipeline (--regional-rr-feature-cutoff). The repo already uses the Satterstrom 2026 cohorts, and clipping is removed.
  4. Choose α with held-out halves on a finer grid that includes values below 0.5. The dispersion optimum for 1e-2 is at the lowest α tested (0.5); outliers start to rise below about 0.625, so α between 0.5 and 0.75 is the range to check.
  5. Without a strength setting, the old DDD+ASD set with Bonferroni at full strength gives the same outliers (222 out-of-fold, 219 with all-chromosome models) but 0.10 higher dispersion. Do not use 1e-4 (the most outliers, 236–240) or a loose cutoff at full strength (overfit).
  6. Expect little further outlier reduction from rr. The remaining ~220 outliers, including the ~130 deficit outliers that no rr option changes, need other explanations (coverage, mappability, flags). Characterizing them is the next step for the outlier rate.

Possible next steps

  • Decide the configuration (Recommendation): the new DNMs with 1e-2 at α ≈ 0.625, or the old DDD+ASD set with Bonferroni at full strength if no strength setting is wanted.
  • Add the strength setting to --compute-rr and fit α on held-out halves over a finer grid (including α < 0.5).
  • Drop chrX/Y masking: rebuild the default table with --compute-rr --apply-rr-safeguards without --mask-features, and run the constraint pipeline without --regional-rr-xy-masked.
  • Characterize the remaining synonymous outliers, especially the ~130 deficit outliers that no rr option changes (coverage, mappability, flags).
  • Then run the constraint pipeline once with the final rr, and compare LOEUF, its deciles, pLI, and outlier flags against unadj.
